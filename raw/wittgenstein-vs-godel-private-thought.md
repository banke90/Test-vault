# Wittgenstein on private language vs. Gödel — am I confusing two things?

[Status: WIP — half-formed]
[Captured: 2026-04-25]

Reading something earlier (can't remember what — that's part of the point of this note, capture it before the trigger decays) made me want to think about whether the private-language argument and Gödelian incompleteness share any deep structure, or whether I'm just pattern-matching on superficial features.

Surface similarity:

- Both are arguments that something purportedly self-contained (a private language; a sufficiently strong formal system) cannot in fact stand on its own — it requires an outside reference (a public language; meta-mathematical statements outside the system).
- Both produce a kind of "you cannot bootstrap this from inside" conclusion.

Why I'm suspicious of the parallel:

- The private-language argument is about *meaning* and the conditions for *rule-following*. Gödel is about *provability* and *truth* in formal systems. These are genuinely different objects.
- The "outside" Wittgenstein needs is the social/public form of life. The "outside" Gödel needs is a stronger formal system (or informal meta-reasoning). Different kinds of outside.
- The private-language argument is contested in philosophy. Gödel is mathematically settled. I should not be surprised if a parallel that "feels right" turns out to be only a structural pun.

What I want to do:

- Re-read the private-language sections of *Philosophical Investigations* (~243–315). I last read these probably a decade ago.
- Read a careful philosophical treatment of any actual proposed analogy. Hofstadter's *Gödel, Escher, Bach* makes a Gödel-meaning connection but is not careful philosophy. Floyd & Putnam on Wittgenstein-on-Gödel might be the right entry.
- Decide whether this is a real connection worth a note, or a pun worth dropping.
